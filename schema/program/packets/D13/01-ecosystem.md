## 0. The shape of the ecosystem in one paragraph

A biotech "event" is almost never born in a database. It is born as a **sponsor disclosure** (press release on a newswire and the company IR page, often but not always mirrored in an SEC 8-K), is **documented** later by a regulator, registry or publisher record with its own clock, is **re-told** by trade press and community, is **priced** by markets and forecasters, and is **re-interpreted** by trackers and roadmaps that bucket it into a field ("senolytics", "partial reprogramming"). The same occurrence therefore has 3–6 artifacts with different dates, different asserters and different wording, and the only stable things are registry identifiers (NCT, NDA/BLA, DOI/PMID, SEC accession). The investor questions D13 serves (what happened, in what order, what led to what, what went wrong, what is a real breakthrough) are answerable only if the graph keeps the occurrence (Event) separate from each artifact that reports or documents it, and keeps "what led to what" as a located assertion rather than as inferred temporal order. *(observation, from sections 1–9)*

## 1. Sponsors: public biotechs and private longevity companies

**Processes and operations.** Sponsors run trials, read them out, file with regulators, raise money and launch. For a listed US company each material step is disclosed by press release and, for many, a Form 8-K whose numbered Items type the event: Madrigal's 8-K of 2022-12-19 carries Items 7.01/8.01 for the MAESTRO-NASH topline readout [S01][S08]; its 8-K filed 2022-12-23 carries Items 1.01 (material agreement), 5.03, 7.01, 8.01 for a financing [S02][S08]; the approval 8-K of 2024-03-15 carries 7.01/8.01 [S05][S08]; earnings are Item 2.02 [S08]. Unity Biotechnology used Item 2.05 (exit costs) and 5.02 (officer departures) when it cut its workforce after its ASPIRE readout [S11][S10], and Items 1.02/3.03 when it dissolved [S10][S12]. *(observation)* The 8-K Item code is a free, structured event-kind signal.

**Not every material event reaches EDGAR.** BioAge Labs announced the discontinuation of its STRIDES Phase 2 trial on 2024-12-06 by press release [S27]; the EDGAR submissions index for BioAge lists no 8-K between 2024-12-01 and 2025-01-31 (only a Form 4 on 2024-12-20) [S09]. *(observation)* An event model that waits for a filing misses harm events; the newswire/IR release is the first and sometimes only primary artifact.

**Private companies disclose only by blog/press release.** Loyal (veterinary longevity, private) disclosed its LOY-002 FDA milestones and a $22M B-2 round in a company blog post whose captured text carries no publication date [S36]; a later post is dated January 13, 2026 and dates the RXE acceptance only relatively ("In February of last year") [S37]. Life Biosciences disclosed IND clearance (2026-01-28) and first participant dosed (2026-06-09) on its own site [S34][S35].

**Causal language lives in sponsor text.** Madrigal's financing 8-K states that "as a result of the Company’s announcement of Phase 3 MAESTRO-NASH clinical trial topline results" a lender confirmed a clinical milestone, making an additional $50 million borrowable [S02]; the CEO quote in the same filing's press release says the financings were "secured following the positive topline readout" [S03]. Unity's 8-K says its operating plan was approved "following the disclosure of full 36-week data from the Company’s ASPIRE study" [S11]; BioAge says the discontinuation "follows observations of liver transaminitis" [S27]. *(observation)* These are exactly the LEADS_TO / RESPONDS_TO statements the vocabulary requires a locator for; "following" is ambiguous between temporal and stated-basis readings and needs a human-or-verifier adjudication of the span.

**Forward-looking milestones are announced before they happen.** Madrigal's 2023-09-13 release states FDA "assigned a Prescription Drug User Fee Act date for resmetirom of March 14, 2024" [S04]; the approval was announced on that date [S05][S06]. The approval release says Rezdiffra "is expected to be available to patients in the U.S. in April" [S06] (a planned launch, not a launch). Loyal wrote that it hoped "to have completed all the regulatory requirements by the end of 2025" [S36]; a January 2026 post reports two of three XCA sections accepted with manufacturing in progress [S37], and a trade article reports Loyal anticipating the final section's review in 2027 [S38]. *(observation)* Planned/guided milestones and their slippage are first-class investor facts and must be stored as dated assertions (scheduled vs occurred), not by overwriting a date.

**Self-description is not a breakthrough judgment.** Madrigal's CEO calls the approval "a historic moment for the NASH field" [S06]; Life Bio calls its IND "the first ever cellular rejuvenation therapy using partial epigenetic reprogramming to reach human clinical trials" [S34]; Loyal calls itself "the likely recipient of both the first- and second-ever RXE acknowledgements from the FDA for longevity drug programs for any species" [S36]. *(observation)* These are ClaimOccurrences (D9) to be tested, not event properties.

## 2. Regulators: what "approved", "designated" and "scheduled" mean, and where each is recorded

**FDA's own definition of breakthrough.** "Breakthrough Therapy designation is a process designed to expedite the development and review of drugs that are intended to treat a serious condition and preliminary clinical evidence indicates that the drug may demonstrate substantial improvement over available therapy on a clinically significant endpoint(s)" [S15]. It is requested by the sponsor, and FDA "will respond to Breakthrough Therapy designation requests within sixty days of receipt of the request" [S15]. The EU counterpart, PRIME, targets "conditions with an unmet medical need" [S18].

**Designations are published only in aggregate, after approval.** FDA's "Breakthrough Therapy Approvals" page says reports listing approvals of breakthrough-designated drugs are "published quarterly for the current calendar year" [S16]; the cumulative PDF (data as of 2025-12-31) states "Total of 337 Approvals" and lists, per row, application number, submission type and number, proprietary name, established name, applicant, approval date and use [S17]. It includes NDA 217785 REZDIFFRA (resmetirom), Madrigal, 14-Mar-2024 [S17], and NDA 215256 Supplement-24 WEGOVY (semaglutide), Novo Nordisk, 15-Aug-2025, for noncirrhotic MASH [S17]. *(observation)* Designation **grant** dates are not in this list; they come only from sponsor releases. A drug that is designated but never approved never appears here.

**Drugs@FDA via openFDA.** The record for NDA217785 gives sponsor MADRIGAL, submissions with type (ORIG/SUPPL), number, status AP, status date 20240314, review priority PRIORITY, class "Type 1 - New Molecular Entity", and application documents (letter and label PDF URLs with dates) [S13]. Wegovy NDA215256 shows a chain of EFFICACY supplements — 2024-03-08 (S-11, PRIORITY), 2025-08-15 (S-24, PRIORITY) and others — each with letter and label URLs [S14]. *(observation)* The record has no submission (receipt) date, no PDUFA goal date, no accelerated-approval flag and no designation flags; supplemental approvals ARE the "label expansion" milestones on frontiers such as GLP-1 beyond obesity.

**PDUFA dates are sponsor disclosures.** The only primary source captured for a PDUFA goal date is the sponsor's release [S04]; commercial "PDUFA calendars" (MarketBeat, BiopharmaWatch, RTTNews, FDATracker) appeared in search results as aggregators of such dates [S50] (search snippets only; not captured, not used as evidence).

**Accelerated approval carries a built-in future event.** Madrigal's 8-K: "Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in ongoing confirmatory trials" [S05]. *(observation)* An accelerated approval creates a pending confirmatory milestone that investors and patients track (risk of withdrawal).

**Veterinary pathway as a longevity precedent.** Loyal describes FDA CVM's Expanded Conditional Approval (XCA) with three technical sections — RXE (effectiveness), TAS (safety) and manufacturing — and reports RXE and TAS accepted [S37]; its site lists "RXE ... Accepted, TAS ... Accepted, CMC ... In progress" (search snippet [S50b]). *(observation)* Section acceptances are regulator acts disclosed only by the sponsor; no public FDA record was found in this stage.

All participant visits have been completed." and last update posted 2025-03-13 [S19]. ## 4. The scholarly record: publications, corrections, retractions

The 2006 Nature paper reporting Aβ*56 (DOI 10.1038/nature04533, PMID 16541076) is indexed by PubMed with article types including "Retracted Publication" [S25]. Crossref's `updated-by` lists an expression of concern dated 2022-07-14 (source `retraction-watch`) and a retraction dated 2024-06-24 recorded twice — once with source `retraction-watch` (record-id 54364) and once with source `publisher` [S22]; the notice itself (DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07691-8) carries the inverse `update-to` [S23]. The Crossref title is prefixed "RETRACTED ARTICLE:" and the work had `is-referenced-by-count` 2410 at capture [S22]. Crossref states it acquired the Retraction Watch database in September 2023, that retractions appear in the `update-to` field of the REST API, and that the `source` field distinguishes `publisher` from `retraction-watch` [S26]. The retraction note gives the reason (figures showing "signs of excessive manipulation") and each author's position: six agree, "Sylvain Lesné disagrees with the retraction", one did not respond [S24]. *(observation)* A retraction event has one primary origin (the publisher's notice, also deposited by the publisher in Crossref), corroborated by derived records with the same date — the Retraction Watch record (Crossref: the database "contains retractions gathered from publisher websites" [S26]) and PubMed's RetractionIn entry, which points at the same notice [S25] — plus a stated reason and author-level dissent. *(Adversarial-review correction, 2026-10-08: the research stage called the publisher and Retraction Watch "two independent asserters"; the Retraction Watch record derives from the publisher's notice, so independent origins = 1.)* The record is the kernel's (HAS_REVISION_STATUS per authority + SourceSnapshot.revisionKind; kernel M6), which D13 projects as a frontier event only when it is a milestone (vocabulary §5).

Peer-reviewed publication is itself a milestone after topline: Insilico announced rentosertib Phase IIa topline on 2024-11-12 [S39] and the Nature Medicine paper on 2025-06-03 [S40]; Crossref and Europe PMC give DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-03743-2, PMID 40461817, PMCID PMC12353801, first publication 2025-06-03 [S41]. Madrigal's approval release notes MAESTRO-NASH "was recently published in the New England Journal of Medicine" [S06].

## 6. Trackers, roadmaps and frontier-setting programmes

- **Lifespan.io Rejuvenation Roadmap** ("Updated: August 21, 2026") is a curated database of rejuvenation therapies grouped by hallmark of aging, with stage columns Drug Discovery, Preclinical in vitro, Preclinical in vivo, Phase 1, Phase 2, Phase 3, Released, filters for discontinued and inactive programs, and per-program pages; it credits AgingBiotech.info's database [S42]. Entries include "ER-100 Life Biosciences (Opthalmic) Phase 1" and "Cellular reprogramming technology Altos Drug Discovery" [S42]. It is JS-rendered (Tavily got only "Loading ..."; Firecrawl with wait succeeded) and has no ids or API (tool-log #8, #15). *(observation)* This is the closest existing artifact to a "frontier × stage" view, and it is a third-party narrative (a source to cite), not our frontier vocabulary.
- **ARPA-H PROSPR** frames a frontier as a programme: it "aims to identify biochemical and physiological markers and develop assessment tools" and to pioneer trial protocols that "can assess age-associated health outcomes in just three years instead of decades of study" [S46].
- **XPRIZE Healthspan** ($101M, 7-year) defines a quantified goal — therapies that "restore muscle, cognition, and immune function by a minimum of 10 years, with a goal of 20 years, in persons aged 50-80 years, in one year or less" — and announced Top 100 semifinalists and Top 40 Milestone 1 winners ($250,000 each) on 2025-05-12 [S47]. *(observation)* Prize milestones are AWARD/financing events attached to a frontier; the prize definition is a candidate frontier definition source.
- **Identifier anchors.** Wikidata has items for mechanism/modality-level concepts — senolytics (Q19587281), cellular reprogramming (Q68262545), life extension (Q574567), glucagon-like peptide-1 agonist (Q5572286), non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (Q28692677), breakthrough therapy (Q25326544), geroscience (Q15727029) — but the search for "partial reprogramming" returned only article items, no concept [S49]. *(observation)* Frontier-as-goal identity is BellLabs-curated; Wikidata gives close-match anchors only.

## 9. Disconfirmation and harm: how frontiers fail in public

- **Senolytics in humans.** Unity's UBX0101 Phase 2 (knee OA): "There was no statistically significant difference between any arm of UBX0101 and placebo at the 12-week endpoint" [S29]; the CEO added that "the evidence that senescent cells contribute to diseases of aging remains compelling" [S29]. Five years later ASPIRE (UBX1325, DME) "did not meet statistical non-inferiority on the average of weeks 20 and 24, the primary analysis endpoint" (8-K [S11]); the board approved a reduction in force affecting all employees [S11]; the company filed a certificate of dissolution effective 5:00 p.m. ET on 2025-09-26 [S12] and filed a Form 15-12G on 2026-01-02 [S10].
- **GLP-1 beyond obesity.** Novo's evoke and evoke+ (3808 adults) "did not confirm superiority of semaglutide versus placebo in the reduction of progression of Alzheimer’s disease" [S31]; the decision to pursue the indication "was based on real-world evidence studies, pre-clinical models as well as post-hoc analyses" [S31]; biomarker improvement "did not translate into a delay of disease progression" [S31]. On the same frontier, FDA approved Wegovy for MASH as a breakthrough-designated supplement on 2025-08-15 [S17][S14] — one frontier, opposite outcomes, in the same year.
- **Muscle-preserving weight loss / aging biology.** BioAge discontinued STRIDES after "11 subjects in the azelaprag treatment groups were observed to have transaminase elevations" of 204 enrolled [S27]; registry status TERMINATED with whyStopped [S19]; shares "plunged by two-thirds" post-market per BioPharma Dive [S28].
- **Alzheimer's disease modification.** Cassava's ReThink-ALZ "did not meet each of the pre-specified co-primary, secondary and exploratory biomarker endpoints" and the second Phase 3 was discontinued [S33]; the Aβ*56 paper was retracted in 2024 [S24].
- **Liver signals recur across frontiers.** Azelaprag (transaminitis) [S27]; rentosertib Phase IIa lists "abnormal liver function (14.8%)" among the most common drug-related AEs [S39]; the vocabulary records an Intellia clinical hold after Grade 4 liver transaminases (vocab C48–C50). *(observation)* A cross-frontier harm question ("which programmes on any longevity frontier reported liver signals") is answerable only if safety-signal events carry a link to an adverse-effect concept (D5 seam).

## 10. The four lenses

- **Science.** Frontier progress is a ladder of milestone kinds (first-in-human → randomized → pivotal → approval) whose evidence lives in D3 studies and the scholarly record; disconfirmation arrives as failed readouts and retractions [S29][S31][S24]. Registries and Crossref are agent-ready; publisher pages need Firecrawl.
- **Economics.** Financings cluster after readouts and approvals; one sponsor states a basis — the Hercules loan milestone "as a result of the Company’s announcement of Phase 3 MAESTRO-NASH clinical trial topline results" [S02] — while the others are temporal only: the CEO's "financings secured following the positive topline readout" [S03] and the 2024 offering, whose 8-K does not mention the approval [S07] (adversarial-review correction); failures trigger RIFs, strategic reviews and dissolution [S11][S12]; market reactions are reported by press, not filed [S28]; sector-level money is reported by trackers with their own counting rules [S48].
- **Community.** Advocacy and roadmap organizations (Lifespan.io, XPRIZE, ARPA-H) define frontiers and publish curated progress [S42][S46][S47]; forecasters price milestone timing [S43][S44]; community comments mix verifiable facts and speculation [S44].
- **AI.** AI-discovered assets are themselves a frontier narrative (Insilico's "generative AI" framing [S39][S40]); for agents, the bottleneck is not search but locators and clocks: EDGAR HTML has no anchors, openFDA lacks designation and goal dates, registry history is blocked, trade press is paywalled or undated, roadmaps are JS-only (tool-log). The most agent-ready event feeds are EDGAR submissions (`items`), openFDA drugsfda, ClinicalTrials.gov v2, and Crossref `update-to`.
