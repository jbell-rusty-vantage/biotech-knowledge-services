## How the domain works in one paragraph

A measurement in this domain is a chain of distinct things that consumer marketing collapses into one word ("your HbA1c", "your HRV", "your biological age"): a biological referent (glycated hemoglobin, NAD+, IgG glycans), a measurand defined by a terminology when one exists (LOINC 4548-4 is a mass fraction of HbA1c over total hemoglobin in blood [S1]), an orderable test with a lab's own code (Labcorp 001453, Quest 496 [S5][S7]), a procedure run by one lab on one method/instrument/kit (Labcorp names "Roche Tina Quant" and cites the "Tina Quant Hemoglobin A1c Gen.3 - Hemolysate application" package insert; Quest names "Turbidimetric Inhibition Immunoassay" [S5][S7]), a specimen, a calibration traceability claim (NGSP certification of a manufacturer/instrument/reagent-lot combination, which expires after one year [S9]), a reference interval or decision limit printed beside the value [S5][S6], and — for clocks, risk scores and wearable scores — an algorithm whose version is usually not stated [S24][S25][S28][S39]. The comparability question ("can these two numbers share an axis") is answerable only when enough of that chain is published, and the survey below shows how often it is not.

## Actors, grouped by type

### A. Terminology and metrology bodies

**Regenstrief Institute / LOINC.** Process: curates observation codes with a six-part model (component, property, time, system, scale, method) and releases versions; each term page states first/last release and change reasons (4548-4: "Last UpdatedVersion 2.73 (MIN)"; class moved from HEM/BC to CHEM in 2.70) [S1]. Artifacts: term pages, part pages, panel membership, LOINC groups, mapping guidance. Notable content: LOINC deliberately keeps HbA1c method-free and instead encodes the standardization protocol (NGSP vs IFCC vs JDS/JSCC), stating "All three protocols produce different numeric values" [S1]; parts link out to protein identifiers (ApoB part LP15400-2 cites UniProt P04114) [S2], which is a ready bridge from a D4 biomarker to a D2 molecular entity. Capturable: yes (public HTML per term). Agent-ready: partial — the term pages scrape cleanly [S1][S2]; the official FHIR terminology server redirected (HTTP 302) for an unauthenticated `$lookup`, so programmatic access needs a LOINC account (readiness finding); the NLM Clinical Tables API is open JSON but returned null SYSTEM/SCALE/UNITS fields for 4548-4 (partial part model) [S3]. Coverage gap that drives the model: the NLM LOINC index returned no term for "RMSSD", "epigenetic", "DNA methylation age" or NAD+ as a metabolite (the "NAD" hits are NAD-dependent enzyme activities and MTHFR genotypes), while HRV SDNN exists (80404-7, 112429-6) [S3]. Consumer longevity metrics therefore mostly need BellLabs-defined `Metric`s with verbatim definitions, not LOINC codes.

**UCUM (via NLM).** Units resolve programmatically: `mmol/mol`, `a` (year), `umol/L`, `ms` [S4]. Agent-ready (open JSON). UCUM gives units, not measurands: NGSP % and IFCC mmol/mol are different protocols, not unit conversions of one value [S1].

**NGSP (HbA1c standardization).** Process: certifies manufacturer methods (instrument, reagent lot, calibrator lot, assigned values, secondary reference lab) against the DCCT reference; "the certificate of traceability expires after 1 year", and "Traceability to the DCCT applies only to results from fresh blood samples" [S9]. Publishes a method-by-variant interference table with an explicit criterion (">±6% at 6 and/or 9% A1C") [S8]. Artifacts: certified methods PDF (updated monthly, "updated 10/26"), certified laboratories PDF, interference table [S8][S9][S10]. Notable rows: Roche "TQ HbA1c Gen. IV whole blood on cobas c513 (HbA1c/Hb*91.5+2.15)" certified Jan '26 with reagent and calibrator lots; consumer test company LetsGetChecked is listed with "ImPress A1c on Roche Cobas c513" (May '26) [S9]; "Ortho-Clinical Vitros … Yes >5% HbF" and "Tosoh G8 ver. 5.24, 5.28" show that software version and method decide interference [S8]. Capturable: yes; agent-ready: medium (PDF tables parse to markdown with Firecrawl; no API).

**Biomarkers of Aging Consortium (BAC) and the validation literature.** Process: consensus frameworks and open challenges, not standards with codes. Moqri et al. state that "no consensus exists on how biomarkers of aging should be validated before their translation to the clinic" [S21] and that "the current lack of standards and consensus on the properties of a reliable aging biomarker hinders their further development" [S22]; BAC runs an open competition on a DNAm dataset of 500 individuals [S23]. Artifacts: reviews, challenge results. Capturable: yes (PubMed/PMC). These are the authorities a comparability or validity assessment cites; they do not emit identifiers.

### B. Regulators and public registries

**FDA CDRH (devices and IVDs).** Processes: 510(k) clearance, recalls, adverse-event intake (MAUDE), safety communications, CLIA complexity categorization. Artifacts and fields, read end to end:
- 510(k) records (openFDA) — `k_number`, `device_name`, `applicant`, `decision_date`, `decision_description`, `product_code`, `clearance_type`, `advisory_committee_description`, `openfda.device_class`, `openfda.regulation_number`. Four Stelo records: K234070 (2024-03-05, Traditional, product code SAF), K260935 (2026-06-12, Traditional, SBH), K261359 (2026-05-21, Special, SAF), K262472 (2026-08-11, Special, SBH); all class 2, regulation 862.1355 [S12]. One consumer device model therefore has four clearances and two product codes in 30 months — device identity and regulatory state are separate, versioned things.
- Recall records — `res_event_number`, `event_date_initiated`, `recall_status`, `product_description` (with REF and UDI-DI), `reason_for_recall`, `root_cause_description`, `product_code`, `k_numbers`, `recalling_firm`. FreeStyle Libre 3 Sensor REF 72081-01, event 98049, initiated 2025-11-24, "Open, Classified", reason "Due to a manufacturing process issue that may cause carbon build up in the sensor that may result in incorrect low glucose results", root cause "Process control", K212132; an earlier 2023 app recall (Android 13 signal loss, root cause "Software design") [S13]. Recalls attach to a model + K number + UDI, sometimes to an app version — the harm path for wearable/CGM metrics.
- MAUDE counts — Stelo: Malfunction 2448, Injury 156, Other 7 (2,611 reports; data last updated 2026-09-29) [S14]. Aggregates only; reports are unverified by design.
- Safety communications — the 2024-02-21 notice that "The FDA has not authorized, cleared, or approved any smartwatch or smart ring that is intended to measure or estimate blood glucose values on its own", applies to any brand, and describes the harm mechanism (wrong insulin/sulfonylurea dosing → hypoglycemia, "coma, or death within hours") [S11]. Fields: date issued, audience recommendations, device description, FDA actions, affected devices (a class of claims, not a model list).
- CLIA categorization database, waived-analyte list and OTC database — monthly updates; searchable by test system, analyte, complexity, specialty and date [S15]. Agent-ready: low — the CLIA search is an HTML form (`Analyte_Name`, `Clia_Complexity`, `Document_Number` …) with no JSON API; a direct GET of the results page returned page chrome only (readiness 1).

**CMS (CLIA program).** Labs are certified per specialty; FDA categorizes tests as waived or moderate/high complexity; labs may be accredited by HHS-approved organizations with stricter requirements [S16]. Consumer vendors advertise "CLIA-certified lab" without a CLIA number on the captured pages [S24][S30] — the certificate is an investor/harm-relevant fact the graph cannot yet resolve (no public CLIA lab API exercised).

**Federal Register / LDT policy.** FDA's May 6, 2024 rule extending device oversight to laboratory-developed tests was vacated by the E.D. Tex. on March 31, 2025 (Am. Clinical Lab'y Ass'n v. FDA, No. 4:24-CV-479-SDJ), and the 2025-09-19 final rule reverted 21 CFR 809.3(a), removing "including when the manufacturer of these products is a laboratory" [S17]. Consequence for D4: most consumer longevity tests (clocks, intracellular NAD+) are LDTs run in a vendor's own CLIA lab, outside FDA premarket review again; "regulatory status as of date" changed twice in 17 months (OPEN-QUESTIONS Priority-1 #4). The Federal Register API is agent-ready (JSON with abstract, dates, citation, raw text URL).

**ClinicalTrials.gov.** Outcome measures name biomarkers and clocks in free text. NCT07220473 (tirzepatide, UTMB, started 2026-02-27) measures "Change in biological age as measured by DNA methylation-based aging clocks (DNAmAge, DNAm PhenoAge, DNAm GrimAge, and DunedinPACE)" — no algorithm version, array or lab [S33]. Nine registered studies mention DunedinPACE in outcomes, from tirzepatide and fecal microbiota transplant to cash transfers and parenting interventions [S34]. NCT02921659 (the NR trial) registers adverse events, FMD, PWV, cognition and CRP/cytokines, but not NAD+, although the paper's headline result is PBMC NAD+ [S31][S32] — registry and publication disagree on which biomarkers were outcomes (D3 seam). Agent-ready: high (v2 JSON API, no auth).

### C. Clinical reference laboratories

**Labcorp.** Test page fields read end to end for 001453 [S5]: test number, CPT (83036), synonyms, methodology ("Roche Tina Quant"), turnaround ("Within 1 day"), sample report link, use, limitations (HbF >10 % lowers results; hemoglobin variants; shortened erythrocyte survival), specimen (whole blood; 4 mL; minimum 0.5 mL), container (EDTA, lithium heparin or sodium fluoride tubes), stability (14 days room temperature/refrigerated/frozen), "Reference Range" (4.8–5.6 % plus prediabetes 5.7–6.4 %, diabetes ≥6.5 %, glycemic control <7.0 %), causes for rejection, footnotes (package insert "Version 1.0: 2017-06"; PubMed ids), and a LOINC map (order 001453 → 4548-4; result 001481 → 4548-4; unit %). Not stated: instrument model, software version, reagent generation in use today (the footnote cites Gen.3; NGSP now lists Gen. IV for cobas c513 [S9]), reference-interval derivation, effective dates. The sample report [S6] carries specimen ID, dates collected/received/reported, result, flag ("High"), units, "REFERENCE INTERVAL" column (4.8 - 5.6), a "Please Note" block with decision limits (Prediabetes 5.7 - 6.4; Diabetes >6.4; glycemic control <7.0), performing lab code "01" with lab name/address/director, "FINAL REPORT", and a report template version ("Enterprise Report Version: 1.00"). Note the page says diabetes "≥6.5%" and the report "Diabetes: >6.4" — the same decision limit printed two ways.

**Quest Diagnostics.** Test 496 fields [S7]: test code, CPT 83036 (with a Medicare limited-coverage notice), clinical significance, methodology ("Turbidimetric Inhibition Immunoassay"), alternative names, specimen requirements (1 mL EDTA whole blood; 0.5 mL minimum; stability 7 days/7 days/6 months; reject sodium fluoride/oxalate and heparinized plasma), reference range "<5.7 %", and "Reference ranges are provided as general guidance only. To interpret test results use the reference range in the laboratory report." LOINC codes and performing lab are hidden until a service area is selected — the captured page cannot answer "which LOINC" or "which lab". Two labs, one analyte: Labcorp accepts sodium fluoride tubes that Quest rejects, stability differs (14 days vs 7 days), and the printed ranges differ in kind (an interval vs a single cutoff) [S5][S7].

### D. Consumer and direct-to-consumer testing services

**Function Health.** "160+ lab tests detecting 1000+ conditions", $365 per year, "Test at 2,000+ labs or we'll come to you", biomarker categories (e.g. "Heart & metabolic 18 Biomarkers", "Cancer & other silent risks 34 Biomarkers" including the Galleri multi-cancer test), physician endorsement quotes [S29]. The captured home page does not name the performing laboratory, methods, LOINC codes or reference intervals. Economics: subscription aggregation of reference-lab tests; harm lens: screening-scale marketing ("detecting 1000+ conditions") with no per-test performance data on the page.

**WHOOP Advanced Labs.** A wearable company now selling blood testing, including Galleri, and "available without a wearable" from August 18, 2026; results "reflect your menstrual cycle" [S39]. Device vendors are becoming test vendors — the Device and LabTest worlds meet in one Organization (D7 seam).

**LetsGetChecked** appears in NGSP's certified list as a manufacturer of an HbA1c application on cobas c513 [S9] — one of the few consumer brands whose assay traceability is publicly certified.

**Jinfiniti (intracellular NAD+).** Product page fields [S30]: product name, at-home finger-prick kit, "CLIA-certified intracellular NAD+ measurement", result unit μM, turnaround ~1 week, price tiers ($198 single; bundles with the company's own NAD+ supplement and a quarterly supplement subscription), an interpretation scale (0–20, 20–30, 30–40 "Suboptimal", 40–100 "Optimal", above 100 μM), precision claim ("3.1% CV"), and claims about origin ("Jinfiniti pioneered NAD testing, established the reference ranges and guidelines"). Measurement compartment: "inside your red blood cells". Missing: method principle, instrument, reference-population derivation, LOINC (none exists [S3]). Conflict structure: the test seller sells the product the test is used to dose, and cites "clinical data — conducted on our own Vitality↑® NAD+ Booster" [S30].

### E. Biological-age and clock vendors

**TruDiagnostic (TruAge).** Product page [S24]: $499 one-time (subscription options), "1,000,000+ DNA markers", finger-prick, "CLIA-Certified Lab", 3–4 week turnaround, reports OMICmAge™ (biological age), SYMPHONYAge™ (11 organ systems), DunedinPACE™, telomere length, 12-cell immune report, "Inflammation Score (CRP & IL-6)", "Epigenetic Biomarkers™ — Clinical biomarkers — proteins, metabolites and clinical measures — estimated directly from your DNA methylation", smoking and alcohol scores; "developed with science from Harvard, Yale, and Duke". Sample report [S25] fields: name, age, sex, ID, collected/reported dates, chronological age, OMICm Age (14.1), Symphony Age (22.6) with 11 organ ages, Pace of Aging (0.7, displayed on a 0.45–1.6 scale), percentiles against "an equal distribution of Harvard research participants and TruDiagnostic clients", epigenetic biomarker percentiles (cystine, mimecan, a GPC lipid) with dietary recommendations, immune cell proportions, DNAm CRP/IL-6 percentiles, "OMICm Fit Age" (grip strength, gait speed — "our least correlated clocks but can be used for most to gamify fitness"), smoking predictor, and "Be sure to retest every 3 months to track your progress." Not stated anywhere in the report: array/platform, algorithm version, lab, normalization, technical-replicate reliability. The word "version" does not occur in the report text.

**Elysium Health (Index).** Saliva test; the science page states "A single sample processed repeatedly can result in epigenetic age estimates that vary up to nine years", that Index "examines global methylation patterns", that "The latest version of Index includes biological ages for nine body systems", and cites Higgins-Chen et al. 2022 as its publication, claiming "agreement across technical replicates within zero to 1.5 years" [S26]. The cited paper describes the PC clocks [S19]; whether Index is that algorithm or a vendor derivative is not stated (OPEN-QUESTIONS Priority-1 #2).

**GlycanAge.** Blood test of IgG glycans quantified "using high-throughput chromatography", converted by "a validated algorithm" into a biological age; the FAQ says "No. GlycanAge is not a diagnostic or medical test." and that single measurements are probabilistic; price ~€499 single [S27]. No algorithm version or reliability statistic on the page.

**InsideTracker (InnerAge 2.0).** Blood-panel clock: 17 biomarkers for males and 13 for females, each adding or subtracting years by deviation from an internal aging trend line; "And as the data evolve, so may the inputs to the InnerAge algorithm." [S28] — an explicitly mutable, unversioned input set.

### F. Academic clock developers (publications as the "reference implementation")

- **DunedinPACE** (Belsky et al., eLife 2022) [S18]: trained on Dunedin cohort Pace of Aging; "Using Illumina EPIC array DNA methylation data from blood collected at age 45"; restricted to probes with test-retest ICC > 0.4 on both 450k and EPIC; "The resulting algorithm included 173 CpG sites"; reliability ICC 0.96 (450k replicates), 0.97 EPIC–EPIC, 0.87 450k–EPIC; "It can be implemented in Illumina 450 k or in EPIC array data"; code on GitHub. Inputs, platform, tissue, output unit (years of aging per year) and reliability are all stated — the publication is the most agent-ready "algorithm version" artifact in the domain.
- **PC clocks** (Higgins-Chen et al., Nature Aging 2022) [S19]: technical replicates of 36 blood samples; Horvath1 replicate deviation median 1.8 years, maximum 4.8; other clocks median 0.9–2.4, maxima 4.5–8.6 years; GrimAge ICC 0.989; longitudinal CpG-clock trajectories deviating "up to 22–57 years off the average trajectory"; PC clocks retrained on principal components are more reliable. This is the disconfirmation source for single-timepoint change claims.
- **GrimAge2** (Lu et al., 2022) [S20]: version 2 of GrimAge, "trained on individuals aged between 40 and 92", adds DNAm estimators of logCRP and logA1C; also applies to saliva. Note: "DNAm logA1C" is an inferred surrogate of the same biomarker LOINC 4548-4 measures [S1][S20] — two Metrics, one Biomarker, different result kinds.

### G. Wearable and CGM makers (devices as instruments)

- **Oura.** OpenAPI 1.41 (75 schemas) [S35]: sleep records expose `sleep_algorithm_version` ("Version of the sleep algorithm used to calculate the sleep data") with enum v1 = "original aka legacy aka OSSA 1.0", v2 = "latest sleep algorithm"; `average_hrv` is described only as "Average heart rate variability during sleep." (no method: RMSSD or SDNN not stated); `average_heart_rate` and `lowest_heart_rate` carry the note that the API value differs from what the app shows; `PublicDailyCardiovascularAge.pulse_wave_velocity` is "derived from vascular age, with possible offset added"; readiness contributors (hrv_balance, resting_heart_rate, sleep_balance …) are 1–100 sub-scores; `ring_id` is deprecated. The sleep staging algorithm 2.0 was announced 2022-11-16 with "79% agreement" with PSG for four stages, based on a 2021 Sensors paper [S36]. Cardiovascular Age: Gen3 or later with membership; "Although Oura Ring does not directly measure your PWV, your photoplethysmograph (PPG) waveform is used to estimate your CVA"; needs 14 nights in 30 days to calibrate; levels Below/Aligned/Above [S37]. Oura is the only vendor surveyed that versions an algorithm per record.
- **WHOOP.** API recovery records carry `score_state`, `user_calibrating`, `recovery_score`, `resting_heart_rate`, `hrv_rmssd_milli`, `spo2_percentage`, `skin_temp_celsius` [S38]; the OpenAPI document defines `hrv_rmssd_milli` as "The user's Heart Rate Variability measured using Root Mean Square of Successive Differences (RMSSD), in milliseconds." [S44] (the rendered page [S38] shows the field only in example payloads). The changelog [S39] records two heart-rate algorithm changes in 2026 without version labels: February 25 ("A new signal processing algorithm improves heart rate tracking across a range of conditions … the data behind your Recovery, Strain, and Sleep is more precise") and July 6 ("WHOOP has a new heart rate algorithm … Because Strain is calculated from heart rate, some members may notice their Strain shift …"; "This happens automatically. No action needed."). Firmware versions are visible to users (e.g. MG "50.30.3.0") but notes are not published — a member writes "I can not see what is fixed or addressed in the update" [S40].

## The four lenses

**Science.** The domain's scientific core is measurement validity: protocol vs unit (NGSP/IFCC [S1]), method-specific interference (HbF, variants [S5][S8]), technical noise of clocks (replicate deviations up to 8.6 years [S19]), compartment/specimen (NAD+ measurable in PBMCs but "undetectable in plasma and urine" in the NR trial [S31], vs a consumer test measuring red cells [S30]), and measured vs inferred (DNAm logA1C vs HbA1c [S20]; Oura PWV "derived from vascular age" [S35]; TruDiagnostic "Epigenetic Biomarkers™" estimated from methylation [S24]). Validation consensus for aging biomarkers is explicitly absent [S21][S22].

**Economics.** Consumer testing is a subscription and bundle business: Function Health $365/yr [S29], TruAge $499 or annual 4-test subscriptions [S24], GlycanAge ~€499 [S27], Jinfiniti $198 with supplement subscriptions [S30]; wearable companies add lab testing (WHOOP Advanced Labs incl. Galleri) [S39]. Clinical reference labs bill by CPT (83036 for HbA1c at both labs) [S5][S7]. Regulatory economics: the LDT vacatur keeps LDT-based consumer tests out of FDA premarket review [S17]; OTC CGMs enter via 510(k) with repeated special 510(k)s [S12]. Investor-relevant events (clearances, recalls, rule changes) are public and dated [S12][S13][S17].

**Community.** Biohackers retest frequently on vendor advice ("retest every 3 months" [S25]; "Test, Act, and Retest" [S30]); members reverse-engineer firmware changes in forums [S40]; consumer names collapse distinct metrics ("HRV" SDNN vs RMSSD [S44][S41]; "biological age" across DNAm, glycan and blood-panel clocks [S24][S26][S27][S28]). Recommendations and endorsements (physicians, universities) travel with products [S24][S29].

**AI / agent readiness.** Agent-ready (3): openFDA 510(k)/recall/event, Federal Register API, ClinicalTrials.gov v2, Europe PMC full text, PubMed MCP, NLM Clinical Tables (LOINC subset, UCUM), Oura OpenAPI JSON, Apple doc JSON. Scrape-clean with stable locators (2): LOINC term pages, Labcorp test pages and sample PDFs, NGSP PDFs, vendor product pages, FDA safety communications, WHOOP changelog. Partial/blocked (1): Quest directory (service-area gating), FDA CLIA database (form only), LOINC FHIR server (account), NCBI Bookshelf (reCAPTCHA blocked Firecrawl and curl for the FDA-NIH BEST glossary), vendor reports (sample PDFs only; real reports are behind accounts and are private personal data, out of scope). No source emits an algorithm-version identifier except Oura's `sleep_algorithm_version` [S35] and publications.

## What the survey implies for the model (handed to `_notes/research-notes.md`)

1. The measurand chain (Biomarker → Metric → LabTest → AssayVersion → ReferenceIntervalVersion) is real and visible in the artifacts, but most links are N; "not reported" must be a value, and comparability must default to UNKNOWN.
2. Algorithm versions matter more than devices for consumers: clocks, wearable scores and HR algorithms change without labels [S28][S39]; the version node must allow "unversioned, pinned by retrieval date" (the existing `SERVICE_ENDPOINT_UNVERSIONED` basis).
3. Devices are instruments for D4 and products for D6; their regulatory records (510(k), recalls) belong to D8 but are the harm path for D4 metrics.
4. The compound → mechanism → biomarker → test path is buildable from public data for NR → NAD+ metabolism → NAD+ (PBMC, LC-MS) → a purchasable test measuring a different compartment — which is itself the most useful answer (a comparability warning).
