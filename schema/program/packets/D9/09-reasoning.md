### Modeling conventions D9 fixes for the claim layer (consumed by fixtures and by D5/D7)

1. **Recommendation shape** (directive 3.9 + D5 seam rule): `ClaimOccurrence {speechAct: RECOMMENDS}`, subject = the recommended means, predicate = a D5 target predicate with object = Target when a goal is stated, else `RECOMMENDS_USE`; dose and population in qualifiers/literals (population `UNSPECIFIED` when unstated); asserter = Person, Organization or ACCOUNT Channel (admitted by the kernel); container via OCCURS_IN; financial interest reachable through D7 episodes, each read with its OVERLAP verdict at the container's `publishedAt` (03 Conventions). A practice report (`REPORTS_PRACTICE`, `TAKES`) is never a recommendation.
2. **In-container disclosure needs no new record**: the speaker's on-air statement of a role ("I'm still their scientific lead guy") is the D7 HOLDS_ROLE authorizing assertion located in a rendition of the same work; "disclosed in the same container" is a path test (03#CQ-CL-05) plus D7's DISCLOSURE TIMING rule: the disclosing rendition must be dated at or before T, else DISCLOSED_IN_LATER_REVISION.
3. **Re-cuts**: one occurrence per act of asserting; it OCCURS_IN the work where the act was first published; the re-cut's spans are extra HAS_EVIDENCE locators on renditions of the re-cut, which carries `republicationKind` and a predicate-only REPUBLISHES Assertion to the original. OCCURS_IN is written from the capture the occurrence was extracted from (the extractor's container); when a later REPUBLISHES match shows that capture was a re-cut, the occurrence's OCCURS_IN is corrected by supersession to the original. Choosing "first published" is therefore a judgment exercised through supersession, not a stored assertion on the edge (see Risks).
4. **Corrections**: the publisher's correction is its own occurrence (asserter = publisher) in the same work, `predicate CORRECTS`, linked to the corrected occurrence by kernel DERIVED_FROM (interim).

One of eight D9 edges carries a valid-time episode. Every valid-time read uses the OVERLAP rule (03 Conventions, D7's rule): a null bound is unknown, never ongoing, and the verdict OVERLAPS / DISJOINT / UNKNOWN is returned and recorded as the `temporalOverlap` facet instead of filtering rows out. The D7 interest edges D9 reads (HOLDS_ROLE, HOLDS_STAKE, RECEIVES_VALUE_FROM) are episodes in D7.

## Risks

- **Kernel dependencies decide the claim layer.** The K model stage settled retellings (K-1: DERIVED_FROM with CITED_FROM_PRIOR_WORK, M5) and typed findings (K-4: `conclusionCode` + `facets`, M9); A1's adversarial review admitted ACCOUNT channels as asserters (K-6), added THIRD_PARTY_ANECDOTE / MECHANISM_REASONING (K-7) and registered the `segment:` and `rendition:` qualifier prefixes (K-5, K-9). Still interim: correction and qualification (K-2), assessing an occurrence (K-3), DISCOURSE_METRIC (K-10), locator offset basis (K-11), and the `credited-name:` qualifier. If the kernel declines them, CQ-CL-03/04/06/08 and Q-D9-04/09/10 keep working but through string conventions that GraphQL clients filter less well.
- **OCCURS_IN is a judgment exercised through supersession** (adversarial review). "The work in which the act was first published" is chosen when a re-cut is recognised; until then the occurrence OCCURS_IN the work it was extracted from. A wrong container flips T and the independence count; it is corrected by superseding the occurrence when a REPUBLISHES Assertion is accepted, never by editing the edge.
- **Archive captures can be substituted** (adversarial review). A Wayback `id_` request served the 2021-08-16 capture for the 2021-12-09 row, with HTTP 200 and byte-identical on re-fetch; the fixtures stage read Levels as absent at T from it. Every archive snapshot now needs its served `memento-datetime` and payload SHA1 checked against the CDX row (11-open-questions F-5).
- **Chunk filter keys can go stale.** `Chunk.workUid` / `channelUid` are copies of the RENDITION_OF and PUBLISHES joins for in-index vector filtering; the chunker must re-stamp them whenever a supersession touches the snapshot's Source, or filtered vector queries return chunks under the wrong work (11-open-questions C-10).
- **Rendition joins are judgments.** Linking a YouTube video, an RSS item and a web page as renditions of one work is usually obvious but sometimes wrong (re-uploads, regional cuts, "Essentials" re-cuts with the same title); RENDITION_OF is structural to keep the hot join cheap, so errors are fixed by supersession, not by an episode.
- **Container time T is the publication time**, but recorded speech may predate publication (interviews recorded months earlier); the interest test uses `publishedAt` unless the work states a recording date (qualifier). A mis-set T flips "valid at the time" findings.
- **Brand vs Organization sponsors**: a sponsor read names a brand; the interest test needs the owning organization through D7 OWNS_BRAND, which may be unknown or contested (NMN.com, Immortals ownership not established).
- **Chunk volume**: one chunk per ~300 tokens of every transcript is the largest node population in the graph; chunks must stay out of GraphQL listing paths that are not paginated, and re-chunking must replace (projection rebuild per chunkerVersion), not multiply.
- **Capture terms**: X and YouTube content was captured by scraping without official APIs; Reddit is blocked. Coverage answers (Q-D9-16) must say so.
