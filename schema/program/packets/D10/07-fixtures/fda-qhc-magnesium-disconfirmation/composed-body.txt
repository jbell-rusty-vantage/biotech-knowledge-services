# Evidence report: magnesium and reduced risk of high blood pressure (fixture draft, not published)

The FDA letter is an external evidence review; this draft re-publishes its outcome with BellLabs citations. Not a BellLabs publication and not medical advice. Bracketed ids are the report-ledger citations.

## Claim under test

A 2016 petition proposed a qualified health claim describing the evidence that diets with adequate magnesium may reduce the risk of high blood pressure as "supportive but inconclusive" [a-petitioner-strength].

## Evidence against

FDA found the evidence inconclusive because the results of the studies are inconsistent [a-fda-strength] [s-strength]. FDA stated that calling the evidence "supportive" overstates its strength [a-fda-strength]. FDA listed three qualified claim wordings for which it would consider enforcement discretion, each describing the evidence as inconsistent and inconclusive or not conclusive, for example "Inconsistent and inconclusive scientific evidence suggests ..." [a-qualified-wording] [a-qualified-wording-2] [a-qualified-wording-3].

## Verdict

Verdict on the claim as petitioned: MIXED_OR_CONFLICTING; the "supportive" characterization does not survive review [s-strength].

## Harms and avoidance

For dietary supplements bearing the claim, FDA intends to consider, as a factor in its enforcement discretion, whether they provide no more than 350 mg magnesium combined when used according to their labeling [a-350-condition].

## Missing or disputed

The letter gives the whole range of magnesium intakes studied as "181 mg/day to 960 g/day", while elsewhere it gives 181 mg/day to 960 mg/day for the studies that showed a benefit and 240 mg/day to 720 mg/day for those that did not, so the upper bound is 960 mg/day and "g/day" is a unit slip inside the letter [a-unit-slip] [a-dose-range-benefit] [a-dose-range-nobenefit] [s-fidelity-unit]. A government fact sheet describes the outcome as an FDA approval, while the letter describes enforcement discretion [s-fidelity-status].
