# K — ecosystem survey: provenance, verification, identity and time

A1, research stage (Wave 1), 2026-10-08. The kernel is not a market; its "ecosystem" is the set of actors whose practice decides whether a statement in our graph can be traced to captured bytes, attributed to one asserter, judged, re-judged when the record changes, and tied to the run that wrote it. This survey is grouped by actor type; for each it records the process, the artifacts produced, whether the artifact is a capturable source, how agent-ready it is (0 blocked · 1 scrape-only · 2 API without locators · 3 agent-ready with stable ids and locators), and what the kernel takes from it. Section 9 interviews the artifact types (≥ 2 real instances each, fields listed). Section 10 reads the whole across the four lenses.

Citation form: `URL · title · retrieved 2026-10-08` (web) or `path · title · read 2026-10-08` (internal contracts). Raw captures and their sha256 are in scratch (`scratchpad\ft\K\raw`, `MANIFEST.sha256`); tools in `_notes/tool-log.md`.

## 1. Standards bodies for provenance and annotation (the vocabulary the kernel borrows)

### 1.1 W3C PROV (PROV-O)

- Process: a W3C Recommendation (30 April 2013) expressing the PROV Data Model in OWL2; terms are grouped into Starting Point, Expanded and Qualified categories. https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/ · PROV-O: The PROV Ontology · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- What it says that the kernel relies on (verbatim from the capture): activities "during their lifespan can **use** and **generate** a variety of Entities (described with prov:used and prov:wasGeneratedBy, respectively)"; "A prov:wasInformedBy relation between Activities suggests that the informed Activity used an Entity that was generated by the informing Activity, but the Entity itself is unknown or is not of interest"; "Provenance chains comprising only Entities can be formed using the prov:wasDerivedFrom property"; "An Agent's responsibility for an Activity or Entity is described using the properties prov:wasAssociatedWith and prov:wasAttributedTo". Subproperties of wasDerivedFrom: wasQuotedFrom, wasRevisionOf, hadPrimarySource; `prov:Bundle` "is a named set of provenance descriptions, which may itself have provenance". Same source.
- Kernel use: the Wave 0 lineage spine is PROV's starting-point subset exactly — `WAS_GENERATED_BY`, `WAS_ASSOCIATED_WITH`, `WAS_INFORMED_BY`, `DERIVED_FROM` — with `used` cut (09-reasoning). PROV's own definition of wasInformedBy (the informing activity generated something the informed one used, identity not of interest) is precisely the evidence-mission → vetting-activity link of directive 3.4. wasRevisionOf / hadPrimarySource are carried as properties (`revisesSnapshotUid`, primary-source status in a SOURCE_AUTHORITY assessment) rather than edges.
- Agent-readiness: the spec is a document, not data; capturable (3) as markdown.

### 1.2 W3C Web Annotation Data Model (selectors and states)

- Process: W3C Recommendation 23 February 2017. https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/ · Web Annotation Data Model · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Selectors defined: Fragment, CSS, XPath, Text Quote, Text Position, Data Position, SVG, Range (§4.2.1–4.2.8). Text Quote: "exact … A copy of the text which is being selected, after normalization. Each TextQuoteSelector MUST have exactly 1 exact property"; prefix/suffix "SHOULD have exactly 1". Text Position: "The text MUST be selected and normalized in the same way as for the Text Quote Selector before counting the number of characters". States (§4.3): "Web resources change over time, and a State might be used to describe how to recover the intended previous version"; TimeState carries `sourceDate` or `sourceDateStart`/`sourceDateEnd` and `cached` "A link to a copy of the Source resource's representation"; "States MUST be processed before processing Selector or Style information". Motivations include `assessing`, `classifying`, `commenting`, `describing`, `editing`. Same source.
- Kernel use: confirms Wave 0's two rules — a locator is only meaningful against a specific captured representation (our `SourceSnapshot` = TimeState + `cached`), and quote + position selectors must share one normalization (verification.v1 `TextNormalizationSchema`, `TextOffsetBasisSchema`). The kernel `SelectorKind` (verification.v1's 12 kinds + WHOLE_SNAPSHOT) is a superset of Web Annotation's text/position/fragment selectors with domain kinds (`pdf_text`, `media_timecode`, `api_record`, `table`).

### 1.3 Nanopublications (assertion + provenance + publication info as one signed unit)

- Process: community guidelines. "A nanopublication consists of an assertion, the provenance of the assertion (simply called 'provenance'), and the provenance of the whole nanopublication (called 'publication info')"; "Nanopublications may be used to expose quantitative and qualitative data, as well as hypotheses, claims, negative results, and opinions that usually go unpublished"; integrity: "Trusty URIs [5] are the recommended way of assigning integrity keys to nanopublications". https://nanopub.net/guidelines/working_draft/ · Nanopublication guidelines (working draft) · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Operations today: the Knowledge Pixels registry reported `nanopubCount: 93615` and 732 agents at the research stage (2026-10-08). Re-read in the adversarial review (`GET https://registry.knowledgepixels.com/` as JSON, 2026-10-09T01:46Z UTC): `trustStateCounter: 35`, `lastTrustStateUpdate: 2026-10-09T01:34:24Z`, `nanopubCount: 93620`, `agentCount: 732`, `accountCount: 831`. The registry exposes no retained-snapshot figure; the earlier "35 retained snapshots" misread `trustStateCounter` and is corrected here. https://registry.knowledgepixels.com/ · Nanopub Registry · retrieved 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09 (UTC).
- Kernel use: the nanopub split maps onto ours — assertion graph ↔ `Assertion` (+ HAS_SUBJECT/HAS_OBJECT), provenance graph ↔ `HAS_EVIDENCE`→`SourceLocator` + `ASSERTED_BY`, publication info ↔ `WAS_GENERATED_BY`→`Activity` (creator, created, template = method). Two differences to keep: (a) nanopubs are immutable and retract by publishing a new signed nanopub of type `npx:retracts` (instance in §9.7) — the same "nothing is deleted; supersede by a new record" rule as the kernel's `supersedesUid` / `recordedTo`; (b) nanopub provenance points at an experiment or a URL, rarely at a located span — the kernel is stricter (span on a snapshot).
- Agent-readiness 3 (signed TriG over HTTP), but domain coverage for longevity is thin; a source to monitor, not a dependency.

### 1.4 Micropublications (claims with their argument)

- "The minimal form of a micropublication is a statement with its attribution. The maximal form is a statement with its complete supporting argument, consisting of all relevant evidence, interpretations, discussion and challenges brought forward in support of or opposition to it"; it claims a role "where simpler, formalized and purely statement-based models, such as the nanopublications model, will not be sufficient". Clark T, Ciccarese PN, Goble CA. J Biomed Semantics 2014. PMID 26261718 · DOI 10.1186/2041-1480-5-28 · PMC4530550. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26261718/ · Micropublications: a semantic model for claims, evidence, arguments and annotations in biomedical communications · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: the minimal form (statement + attribution) is our `Assertion` + `ASSERTED_BY` + located span; the maximal form (support and challenge) is what an evidence mission produces — an `Assessment` / `Report` that `DERIVED_FROM` the supporting and challenging assertions. We do not need a micropublication node; we need the challenge path to be first-class, which directive 3.4 already demands.

### 1.5 SEPIO (Scientific Evidence and Provenance Information Ontology) and its LinkML successor

- The OWL repository now warns that it "is out of date … PLEASE USE THE MODELS IN THE NEW REPOSITORY". https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monarch-initiative/SEPIO-ontology/master/README.md · SEPIO-ontology README · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- SEPIO-LinkML core classes (verbatim definitions): Proposition — "An abstract entity representing a possible fact that is either true or false"; Statement — "A claim of purported truth as made by a particular agent, on a particular occasion"; EvidenceLine — "An independent, evidence-based argument that may support or refute the validity of a specific proposition"; `direction` — "whether the Statement supports, disputes, or remains neutral w.r.t. the validity of the Proposition it evaluates"; `directionOfEvidenceProvided` (supports, disputes, neutral); `negated` — "A boolean flag set to 'true' to represent a negation of the proposition"; Contribution — "An action or actions taken by a particular agent in the creation, modification, assessment, or deprecation of some entity". https://sepio-framework.github.io/sepio-linkml/ · SEPIO LinkML · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use — a near one-to-one confirmation of the Wave 0 claim lifecycle: SEPIO Proposition = `Claim`; Statement (one agent, one occasion) = claim-occurrence `Assertion`; EvidenceLine (independent argument with direction and strength) = `Assessment` (EVIDENCE_SYNTHESIS / VETTING) with `effectDirection`; Contribution = `Activity` + `Agent`. SEPIO's "independent" evidence line is the concept behind CQ-CL-06 / CQ-AX-05 (independence counting) — see Q-K-21.

### 1.6 Biolink Knowledge Level / Agent Type (KL/AT)

- KnowledgeLevelEnum: "characterizing the type of knowledge expressed in a statement and the kind of evidence and reasoning that supports it … Values include knowledge assertion, logical entailment, prediction, statistical association, text co-occurrence, direct observation"; `observation` = "A statement reporting (and possibly quantifying) a phenomenon that was observed to occur - absent any analysis or interpretation". https://biolink.github.io/biolink-model/KnowledgeLevelEnum/ · KnowledgeLevelEnum · retrieved 2026-10-08. AgentTypeEnum: "distinguish human (manual) agents from automated agents (including data analysis pipelines, computational models, text-mining agents …)". https://biolink.github.io/biolink-model/AgentTypeEnum/ · AgentTypeEnum · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: `AgentKind` already reuses AgentTypeEnum (Wave 0). Knowledge level overlaps `BasisKind` + `assertionKind`; we keep BasisKind (asserter's basis, CQ-CL-02) and do not add a second "knowledge level" axis — a text co-occurrence is not an Assertion at all in our model; it is a `MENTIONS` projection (directive 3.8).

### 1.7 Evidence & Conclusion Ontology (ECO)

- Home page capture (2,295 chars) describes ECO as the ontology of evidence types used in annotation. https://www.evidenceontology.org/ · Evidence & Conclusion Ontology · retrieved 2026-10-08. Kernel use: an evidence-type code is a candidate qualifier on `HAS_EVIDENCE` or on an assessment, owned by D3 (study types); not a kernel node.

### 1.8 schema.org ClaimReview and Google's fact-check markup

- schema.org: "A fact-checking review of claims made (or reported) in some creative work (referenced via itemReviewed)"; `claimReviewed` "A short summary of the specific claims reviewed"; `reviewRating` "The rating given in this review". https://schema.org/ClaimReview · ClaimReview - Schema.org Type · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Google requires `claimReviewed`, `reviewRating`, `url`, `author` (of the fact check, "not the publisher of the claim"), `itemReviewed` typed `Claim`, and recommends `appearance` or `firstAppearance` of the claim; on ratings it says "Different fact-checking projects have a variety of rating schemes which can have subtle differences, particularly for intermediate values". https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/factcheck · Fact Check (ClaimReview) structured data · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: ClaimReview is the industry's claim → occurrence → verdict triple (Claim, `appearance`, `reviewRating`). It confirms our split and gives a mapping problem: third-party textual ratings must be mapped to the verdict lattice by a versioned rule, never stored as our verdict (see §4, Q-K-28).

### 1.9 Wikidata (statements, qualifiers, references, ranks)

- References: "the value is the source. Typically the property used for sources is one of two options: stated in (P248) … and reference URL (P854)"; for web pages add "publication date (P577) … If no publication date is provided use retrieved (P813)", plus archive URL (P1065) and archive date (P2960). https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Sources · Help:Sources · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Ranks: "The *deprecated* rank is used for statements that are known to include errors … or that represent outdated knowledge"; reason via P2241; "All statements, including deprecated ones, must be verifiable in the sense that a source makes the claim whether or not the claim is true"; deprecating rather than deleting "provides a mechanism for representing the evolution of theories and ideas" and keeps Wikidata "a secondary knowledge base (that collects and links to references), rather than a primary database of facts". https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking · Help:Ranking · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: the strongest external precedent for two kernel rules — (1) **capture fidelity is not truth** (a deprecated statement is still verifiable as "a source makes the claim"; our Assertion stays ACCEPTED when a later evidence report contradicts the proposition), and (2) **nothing is deleted** (deprecate/supersede). Wikidata keeps qualifiers on the statement, as we keep qualifiers on the Assertion.

## 2. Scholarly-record integrity infrastructure (publishers, indexers, watchdogs)

### 2.1 Publishers and COPE (who retracts, how)

- COPE guidance: "Notices of retraction should link to the retracted article, clearly identify it with title and authors, and be published promptly and be freely accessible to all readers"; under insufficient information "an editor could consider publishing an expression of concern"; "To minimise harmful effects and uptake (eg, citation of erroneous work, acting on their findings, or drawing incorrect conclusions), retraction notices should be published as soon as the editor is confident". https://publicationethics.org/guidance/guideline/retraction-guidelines · Retraction guidelines (COPE) · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Process artifact: the retraction notice is a **separate publication** with its own DOI and PMID that points at the retracted work (instance §9.1).

### 2.2 Crossref (Crossmark update metadata) and the Retraction Watch database

- Crossmark: "gives readers quick and easy access to the current status of an item of content, including any corrections, retractions, or updates"; members "commit to informing us if there is an update such as a correction or retraction"; "the presence of Crossmark itself is not a guarantee". https://www.crossref.org/documentation/crossmark/ · Crossmark (Crossref documentation) · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- "12th September 2023—The Center for Scientific Integrity, the organisation behind the Retraction Watch blog and database, and Crossref … announced today that the Retraction Watch database has been acquired by Crossref and made a public resource." https://www.crossref.org/blog/news-crossref-and-retraction-watch/ · News: Crossref and Retraction Watch · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Operations observed: `GET api.crossref.org/v1/works?query.bibliographic=resveratrol+SIRT1&filter=update-type:retraction` returned 170 results; each `update-to` entry carries `type` (retraction, erratum), `source` (`retraction-watch` or `publisher`), `record-id` (Retraction Watch) and `updated.date-time`; several notices carry **two** entries (one per source). https://api.crossref.org/v1/works?query.bibliographic=resveratrol+SIRT1&filter=update-type:retraction · Crossref REST API works query · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: correction status arrives from two authorities that can differ — the kernel records each as an Assertion with its own asserter (publisher vs Retraction Watch), and a `SourceSnapshot.revisionKind` (RETRACTION, EXPRESSION_OF_CONCERN, ERRATUM …, Wave 0 enum) on the **new snapshot** of the retracted work. Agent-ready 3. Candidate identifier scheme `RW_RECORD` (Retraction Watch record id; issuer Crossref) for the register.

### 2.3 NLM PubMed (indexer of record)

- The retracted article record gains `PublicationType` "Retracted Publication" and `CommentsCorrections RefType="RetractionIn"`; the notice gets "Retraction Notice" and `RetractionOf`; `DateRevised` moves (2025-07-04 on PMID 29039533). https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=29039533 · PubMed XML record 29039533 · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: PubMed's `History/PubMedPubDate` statuses (received, accepted, pubmed, medline, entrez, pmc-release) are five different clocks on one record — direct evidence for CQ-TM-03 (publication vs observation vs effective vs ingestion time) and for keeping `publishedAt`, `retrievedAt` and recorded time separate on `SourceSnapshot` / `Assertion`.

### 2.4 Full-text hosts (PMC, Europe PMC)

- PMC serves Hubbard et al. 2013 as an NIH author manuscript (`manuscript-id` NIHMS504463, `pmcid-ver` PMC3799917.1) in JATS with paragraph structure. https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=3799917 · PMC JATS record PMC3799917 · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- For Pacholec et al. 2010 (JBC, free on the web) PMC returns only a stub: "The publisher of this article does not allow downloading of the full text in XML form." https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=2832984 · PMC efetch PMC2832984 · retrieved 2026-10-08; Europe PMC `fullTextXML` answered HTTP 500. https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC2832984/fullTextXML · Europe PMC REST · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Kernel use: (a) the author manuscript and the version of record are two renditions — two `Source`s (one retrieval endpoint each) whose equivalence is a D9 container question, not a kernel merge; (b) the locator-grade text guaranteed for every paper is the PubMed abstract; full-text locators exist only where the publisher allows machine reading.

- **Bitemporal databases** as design precedents: XTDB — "'System time' represents the audit history of all changes to records and captures the time that information entered the system. XTDB's WITHOUT OVERLAPS is the integrity rule behind CQ-TM-05 (mutually exclusive states) — an executor check, not a schema element.

## 4. Claim checkers and evidence products (how others publish verdicts and citations)

### 4.1 Science Feedback / Health Feedback (claim reviews)

- Process: an editor "searches the scientific literature for relevant evidence … and invites relevant experts to contribute"; "Where appropriate and possible the editor reaches out to the person who made the claim"; "The final ruling regarding the verdict attributed to the claim is made by a Science Feedback editor based on suggestions by the scientists"; corrections "a note will be added on the original item". Verdict tags by credibility level (Very High: Accurate/Correct; High: Mostly Accurate/Correct; Neutral: Lacks Context, Partially Correct, Imprecise; Low …) and reason tags such as "Inadequate support: Reference used to support the claim is non-existent, of low scientific credibility or insufficient", "Flawed reasoning", "Cherry-picking", "Misrepresents source (Strawman)". https://science.feedback.org/process/ · Science Feedback process and verdicts · retrieved 2026-10-08.
- Artifacts: claim review pages with embedded ClaimReview JSON-LD (instances §9.4). Agent-ready 2 (structured but dirty).
- Kernel use: their pipeline is our evidence mission run by humans: claim → occurrence (`itemReviewed`, author, platform, date) → investigation → verdict + reasons → published report with corrections. Their **reason tags** ("inadequate support", "misrepresents source") are close to verification.v1's `unsupportedFacets` and orthogonal properties; their credibility scale is a lossy projection of the 15-value lattice.

### 4.2 scite (smart citations: supporting / contrasting / mentioning)

Both quoted from the open-access abstract on the publisher's article page: https://direct.mit.edu/qss/article/2/3/882/102990/scite-A-smart-citation-index-that-displays-the · scite: A smart citation index that displays the context of citations and classifies their intent using deep learning (Nicholson JM et al., Quantitative Science Studies 2021;2(3):882–898, DOI 10.1162/qss_a_00146) · retrieved 2026-10-09T01:53Z UTC with Firecrawl (`scrape`, maxAge 0; markdown projection sha256:951b9b9b6c776ac15ac3fcd67807f8b12732929898e9166eed5219a9cd25b695; both quotes occur once). The research stage had taken them from the OpenAlex inverted-index reconstruction (https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1162/qss_a_00146, record W3185077430), which is now only the discovery route (tool-log A6). The product help pages failed to fetch and the blog URL now 404s (tool-log row 3).
- Kernel use: scite's three classes are exactly directive 3.8's MENTIONS / SUPPORTS / CONTRADICTS — and show why the directive is right to place them carefully: scite classifies a **citing sentence (a located span) relative to a cited work**, not a document relative to an entity. Our equivalent: a `SourceLocator` in the citing paper is `HAS_EVIDENCE {role: SUPPORTS|CONTRADICTS}` of an Assertion/Claim; "mentions" is a retrieval projection with no evidential weight.

## 6. The producer: `verification.v1` (Knowledge Services, ai-engineer-knowledge-services)

Read 2026-10-08: `docs/verification/README.md` (Verification Module Guide), `packages/contracts/src/verification/model.ts`, `selectors.ts`, `claims.ts`, `adjudication.ts`.

- Process: five ordered questions — capture integrity, selector integrity, mechanical correctness, semantic support, policy admission; "Later stages may add restrictions; they must not reverse an earlier deterministic failure." Pipeline stages run as worker operations (13 operation kinds, e.g. `verification_capture`, `verification_claims`, `verification_report`, `verification_adjudication`, `verification_audit_bundle`); Mission Control's Temporal `verificationWorkflow` dispatches. ## 10. The four lenses

- **Science.** Scholarly self-correction is slow and two-channelled: the resveratrol–SIRT1 proposition was published as fact in 2003 (Nature, PMID 12939617), qualified in 2005 (JBC, PMID 15684413: "substrate-specific activator"), contradicted in 2010 (JBC, PMID 20061378: "not direct activators of SIRT1"), and re-supported with a mechanism in 2013 (Science, PMID 23471411: "SIRT1 can be directly activated through an allosteric mechanism"); a 2017 paper that presupposed it was retracted in 2025 for image anomalies (PMID 29039533 / 40613201). A useful kernel must hold all five as located occurrences with dates and let an evidence report state a lattice verdict (here plausibly MIXED_OR_CONFLICTING or SUPPORTED_WITH_QUALIFICATION) without deleting any of them. PROV, SEPIO, nanopubs, micropublications and Wikidata all agree on the shape; none of them binds evidence to a located span on captured bytes — that is our differentiator and verification.v1's job.
- **Economics.** The infrastructure that makes provenance cheap is public and free (PubMed E-utilities, Crossref incl. Retraction Watch since 2023, Wikidata, Bioregistry, OpenAlex). The expensive parts are full text (per-publisher machine-reading rights), registry history (not exposed), and human adjudication (verification.v1 has 180 unlabelled review candidates). Interested parties produce most claim occurrences at near-zero cost (vendor catalogs, brand blogs, encyclopedia edits); disconfirmation is produced by a few funded actors (journals, Science Feedback, regulators). The kernel's cost discipline: store digests and spans, not bodies; store verdicts, not bundles.
- **Community.** Biohackers meet claims through retellings (podcasts, brand blogs, reels) far from the primary; fact-checkers re-attach the occurrence (who, where, when) before judging; on Wikidata we observed one promotional edit set removed by another editor about 33 hours after it was made (§5; a single instance, not a measured norm). The community norms worth encoding are attribution (who said it, in which container, when) and retention of the losing side (deprecated rank, retraction notices that link to what they retract). Credential claims of speakers ("neuroscientist") are themselves claims to be checked (Science Feedback did).
- **AI.** LLM agents will extract claims and propose writes; verification.v1 separates producer and verifier deployments, abstains by policy, and measures citation completeness and correctness. scite shows machine classification of supporting / contrasting / mentioning at scale (880 M statements) — useful as a retrieval signal, never as a verdict. The kernel's job for agents is to make the trace cheap to query: one hop from an answer sentence to an assertion, one to a locator, one to a snapshot digest, one to the run that wrote it (Q-K-20, Q-K-03), and the four lineage questions of directive §4 as fixed query patterns.
