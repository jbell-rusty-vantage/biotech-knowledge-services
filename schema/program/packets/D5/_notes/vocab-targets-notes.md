# Targets vocabulary — notes (Wave 0, A5, 2026-10-08)

Companion to `../vocab-targets.yaml` (version 0.1.0). Records the choices, the alternatives rejected, the evidence used to shape the set, and what later D5 stages must carry forward. Research is not medical advice.

## 1. What the owner asked for and what the pinned proposal had

- Directive 3.1: people pursue targets (sleep, strength, brain fitness, body composition, longevity, energy, mood, recovery); the graph must answer "what helps or hurts target T, by what evidence, for whom" and "what is this product or protocol for"; a small curated controlled vocabulary, a node per goal, not free text. Directive 3.2: nothing encodes good/bad as an entity property; always an assessment or assertion with a source and a direction of effect on a target.
- Pinned W16 had two seeds:
  - `FunctionalGoal` (`hu:functional-goal:`): "better sleep", "cardiovascular health"; properties `name`, `description`, `goalType: String` (free string, **no enumerated values**); one edge `OPERATIONALIZED_BY -> Target`; reached from `ProtocolEdition.functionalGoals` (`HAS_FUNCTIONAL_GOAL`, asserted) and from the 144-member `AssertionSubjectTarget` union.
  - `Target` (`hu:target:`): a source-stated numeric/ordinal threshold of a protocol edition for one metric ("plasma lithium 0.6–0.8 mmol/L"); `FOR_METRIC -> Metric`.
- Pinned D2 `Outcome` (module mechanisms): biological or clinical outcome concept (muscle strength, insulin sensitivity, life span) with `OPERATIONALIZED_BY -> Metric`. It is the referent study OutcomeDefinitions point at.
- Pinned `OutcomeDirection` enum (W09): IMPROVED, WORSENED, NO_CHANGE, MIXED, INCONCLUSIVE, UNKNOWN — "direction of change as reported by the source".
- Competency questions touching goals: catalog PC-01 ("what was the person trying to improve at time T" — public goal concept vs private goal version, `UserGoalVersion {functionalGoalUid,…}` in PCS), catalog RC-01 ("why was one product preferred over another for a stated goal"; "a decision is an occurrence, not a `Product-[:RECOMMENDED_FOR]->Goal` edge"), the marketing example "supports healthy sleep" recorded as an assertion with polarity and locator (competency-questions.md line 162), catalog PR-03.

## 3. How the set maps to measurable outcomes (evidence used)

Goal: every target must map to outcomes that evidence products, clinical instruments and wearables already measure, so that "helps T" can be backed by located evidence and not by marketing language alone.

- **Evidence products frame goals as outcome categories.** Examine.com's category index lists (verbatim category names) "Autoimmune Disease, Brain Health, Cancer, Cardiovascular Health, Diabetes & Blood Sugar, Ear, Nose & Throat, Energy & Fatigue, Eyes & Vision, Fat Loss, Gut Health, Healthy Aging & Longevity, Immunity & Infectious Disease, Joints & Bones, Kidney & Urinary Health, Liver Health, Lungs & Breathing, Men’s Health, Mental Health, Muscle Gain & Exercise, Oral Health, Other, Pain, Pregnancy & Children, Skin, Hair, & Nails, Sleep, Women’s Health" [C1]. Ten of our 12 top-level targets have a direct Examine category; recovery and endurance do not (Examine folds them into "Muscle Gain & Exercise"). Examine mixes goals with disease groups (Cancer, Autoimmune Disease) — we keep diseases as Conditions (D2), not targets.
- **Clinical patient-reported outcome frameworks.** PROMIS adult core domains: Ability to Participate in Social Roles & Activities, Anxiety, Cognitive Function, Depression, Fatigue, Pain Intensity, Pain Interference, Physical Function, Sleep Disturbance [C2]. Definitions captured, e.g. Sleep Disturbance: "Perceptions of sleep quality, sleep depth, and restoration associated with sleep."; Physical Function: "Assesses self-reported capability (not performance) of physical activities." [C2]. Maps sleep, brain-fitness, mood, energy, strength.
- **ICF body functions (WHO).** "ICF is the WHO framework for measuring health and disability at both individual and population levels." [C3a]. Codes used in the mapping are copied from the ICF checklist [C3b]: b130 Energy and drive functions, b134 Sleep, b140 Attention, b144 Memory, b152 Emotional functions, b164 Higher level cognitive functions, b410 Heart, b420 Blood pressure, b435 Immunological (allergies, hypersensitivity), b515 Digestive, b525 Defecation, b530 Weight maintenance, b730 Muscle power. ICF codes are carried as mapping metadata (future `Identifier` scheme `ICF` on the Target), not as keys.
- **Healthy-aging function framework (WHO ICOPE intrinsic capacity).** Domains "locomotion, cognition, vitality, psychological well-being, and sensory capacity (hearing and vision)" [C4]. Gives longevity a function-centred decomposition and confirms strength/endurance (locomotion), energy (vitality), mood (psychological), brain fitness (cognition). Sensory capacity is not a biohacking target in 0.1.0 (deferred implicitly; no question).
- **Wearables frame goals as daily scores.** Oura: "Your Readiness Score reflects how balanced your recovery and activity are. It looks at your sleep quality, body signals, and activity levels" and "Readiness, Activity, and Sleep Scores — and their contributors — are rated on a scale of 0-100." [C5]. WHOOP: "WHOOP recovery is a daily measure of how prepared your body is to perform"; inputs HRV, resting heart rate, sleep performance, respiratory rate [C6]. This is why `target:recovery` exists as its own target (distinct from sleep, which is an input) and why vendor scores appear only as example outcomes: they are proprietary composites (D4 owns them as measures with method versions).
- **Clinical outcome classification.** The COMET/Cochrane outcome taxonomy (Dodd et al. 2018) separates physiological outcomes, measures of life impact ("global quality of life or some measure of functioning") and adverse events, and found that only 35% of core outcome sets referenced adverse events [C7]. Two consequences for us: targets are life-impact/functioning goals whose physiological measures are surrogates (D4 `EndpointClass`), and harm must use the same channel as benefit (WORSENS_TARGET), otherwise it gets under-reported exactly as the taxonomy found.
