## 0. The shape of the domain in one paragraph

D8 is a domain of **documents issued by authorities about other things**: a regulator's letter about an ingredient, an inspector's findings about a facility, a lab's certificate about a lot, a certifier's listing about a product and its lots, a court's opinion about patent claims, a company's filing about its suppliers and licences. Almost every D8 "fact" is therefore a **dated statement by a specific issuer with a specific scope**, and the dominant failure mode is scope inflation: a GRAS "no questions" letter read as an approval [S07][S08], a filing read as a capability, a facility certification read as a lot certification [S13], a "Certificate of Analysis" read as a measurement when it certifies "quantity by input" [S18], a patent shown as "Active" when its asserted claims were held invalid [S21][S22], a regulator's exclusion read as permanent when it was reversed three years later [S03][S04]. The modeling consequence (developed in `_notes/research-notes.md`): D8 needs very few node types; it needs **issuer + scope + as-of + verdict** on a small number of record types, and it needs the kernel's assertion/locator chain to carry the rest.

## 1. Regulators and courts (authorities that issue standing)

### 1.1 US FDA — dietary supplements and foods (Human Foods Program, formerly CFSAN)

**Processes and operations**

- **New dietary ingredient notification (NDIN).** A manufacturer or distributor files at least 75 days before marketing a supplement with an NDI; FDA "reviews this information to determine whether it provides an adequate basis" for the safety conclusion; the notification is confidential for 90 days and then posted on regulations.gov under its NDI number [S05]. Acceptance for filing "is a procedural matter" and not a safety finding [S03]. FDA's failure to respond to an NDIN "does not constitute a finding by us that the NDI … is safe or is not adulterated" (21 CFR 190.6(f), as restated in [S03]).
- **Drug-preclusion ("race-to-market") determinations.** FDA can find an article excluded from the dietary-supplement definition when it was authorized for investigation as a new drug before being marketed as a supplement. For NMN, FDA wrote to Inner Mongolia Kingdomway on 2022-10-11: "Accordingly, we conclude that NMN is excluded from the dietary supplement definition under 21 U.S.C. § 321(ff)(3)(B)(ii) and may not be marketed as or in a dietary supplement." [S05]. FDA sent "similar letters to all firms that had submitted NDINs for NMN"; the response cites FDA letters on NDI 1189 (Willy Chemicals), NDI 1247 (SyncoZymes, acknowledged 2022-05-16) and NDI 1259 (Kingdomway, exclusion letter 2022-10-11 and a supplemental letter dated 2022-11-04) [S03].
- **Citizen petitions and reversals.** NPA and ANH petitioned on 2023-03-07 (amended 2024-12-24); FDA's response under docket FDA-2023-P-0872 grants it in part: "we now conclude that NMN is not excluded from the definition of dietary supplement under section 201(ff)(3)(B)" because NMN "was marketed as a dietary supplement in the United States before such authorization" [S03]. The letter is signed by the Principal Deputy Director for Human Foods [S03]. The letter itself prints "September 29, 2029" on page 1 (a typo in the letter, confirmed on the rendered page and in the raw text layer); the date of record is 2025-09-29, from the digital-signature timestamp ("Date: 2025.09.29 09:36:52 -04'00'") and the docket's postedDate [S03][S04] (tool-log E5, corrected in the fixtures stage; re-fetched 2026-10-08 with browser headers: HTTP 200, sha256 371a4b93…).
- **GRAS notification.** Voluntary; FDA closes a notice with a response ("FDA has no questions", or other outcomes) [S07][S08]. The response is explicitly not an affirmation: "This letter is not an affirmation that urolithin A is GRAS under 21 CFR 170.35." and does not address the food–drug prohibition of section 301(ll) [S09].
- **CGMP for supplements (21 CFR Part 111).** Firms "must establish product specifications for the identity, purity, strength, and composition of the finished batch … and for limits on those types of contamination" and component identity specifications [S29]. Inspections end with a Form FDA 483; the firm responds; FDA may issue a warning letter that evaluates the response item by item [S10].
- **Enforcement.** Warning letters (posted on fda.gov with a CMS/MARCS number) [S10][S11][S12]; recalls classified Class I/II/III and published as enforcement-report records with a recall number, event id, status and termination date [S14][S15][S16][S17].

**What is investor- and biohacker-relevant in practice (observed in captures)**

- One firm's chain is visible end to end across three sources: Imu-Tek Animal Health was inspected 2025-10-06 to 2025-10-14, received a Form 483, responded 2025-10-29 [S10]; initiated a recall of Immuno-5 Colostrum Powder lot #216 for undeclared milk on 2025-11-25 (H-0483-2026, Class II, terminated 2026-08-14) [S17]; and received warning letter CMS # 724610 on 2026-06-05 for unapproved new drug claims, misbranding and Part 111 violations, including "Specifically, during the inspection you stated that you have not established finished product specifications for any of your dietary supplements." [S10]. This inspection → 483 → recall → warning letter sequence is the canonical D8 harm chain and the natural seam to D13 events.
- A longevity-marketed product was recalled Class I: "Spermidine, Maximum Strength, 10 mg per serving, Support healthy Aging" lot 12792402-44, 6,465 bottles, undeclared wheat, distributed in the US and Germany (F-0729-2025) [S14].
- Recalls also capture claim violations, not only contamination: Hi-Tech Pharmaceuticals' quercetin recall reason is "Unapproved Drug Claims and Misbranded." (H-0594-2025) [S15]; and process failures: ULTRAtab's melatonin recall reason is "cGMP Deviations" with three bulk lots (F-0728-2022) [S16].

### 1.2 US FDA — drugs and compounding (CDER)

- **Drug approval records** (Drugs@FDA via openFDA): one application (e.g. NDA021083 RAPAMUNE, sirolimus, sponsor PF PRISM CV) carries products and 58 submissions, the original approved 1999-09-15 as "Type 1 - New Molecular Entity" with later CMC and labeling supplements [S23]; NDA217785 REZDIFFRA (resmetirom, Madrigal) ORIG-1 approved 2024-03-14 with letter and label URLs per submission, plus openFDA harmonization (UNII RE0V0T1ES0, NDCs, pharmacologic class) [S24].
- **503B outsourcing facilities**: GenoGenix LLC registered as an outsourcing facility on 2025-02-04, was inspected 2025-07-14 to 2025-07-18, and received warning letter 718739 (2026-01-20) stating that it "compounded drug products using bulk drug substances that are not eligible for the use in compounding under section 503B, including 5-amino-1-methylquinolinium iodide (5-Amino-1MQ) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+)"; labels for products including Epithalon lacked required statements; the firm had initiated a voluntary recall on 2025-07-30 [S11]. This is the biohacker "longevity injectables" channel and it is regulated as drugs, not supplements. Niagen Bioscience states that pharmaceutical-grade Niagen "is manufactured for use by U.S. FDA-registered 503B outsourcing facilities in compounding activities" [S25].
- **Trial registrations as regulatory evidence.** FDA's 2022 NMN exclusion cites ClinicalTrials.gov records of MIB-626 (Metro International Biotech) as the "substantial clinical investigations" [S05]. Verified: NCT04817111 (lead sponsor Metro International Biotech, LLC; first posted 2021-03-25) and NCT05040321 (lead sponsor Brigham and Women's Hospital; "Sirtuin-NAD Activator in Alzheimer's Disease") [S26][S27]. ~~FDA's footnote gives the NCT05040321 URL for both the COVID-19/AKI title and the Alzheimer's title~~ — **corrected in the fixtures stage (2026-10-08):** the PDF text layer and the rendered page give NCT05038488 for the COVID-19/AKI trial, and ClinicalTrials.gov resolves NCT05038488 to exactly that title; the duplicate NCT05040321 came from Firecrawl's PDF parser (tool-log E6; fixture nmn-us-exclusion-reversal). Regulatory status still depends on D3 objects, and identifier verification matters — including verification of our own extraction.

### 1.3 European Union — Commission and EFSA (novel foods)

- **Authorisation by implementing regulation.** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/16 adds nicotinamide riboside chloride to the Union list: applicant ChromaDex Inc. (request 2018-05-10), EFSA consulted 2018-10-08, EFSA opinion adopted 2019-07-04, authorised 2020-02-20 [S30]. The annex carries **conditions of use** (food supplements: 300 mg/day general adult population excluding pregnant and lactating women; 230 mg/day for pregnant and lactating women), **labelling** ("Nicotinamide riboside chloride"), **specifications** (≥ 90 % NRCl; water ≤ 2 %; residual solvents, by-products, arsenic ≤ 1 mg/kg, microbiological criteria) and **data protection**: only ChromaDex may place it on the market during the protection period; "End date of the data protection: 20 February 2025." [S30].
- **Scientific opinion ≠ authorisation.** EFSA's NDA Panel concluded β-NMN "is safe under the proposed conditions of use for the adult population, excluding pregnant and lactating women" (up to 300 mg/day in food supplements; NOAEL 400 mg/kg bw per day from a 90-day rat study, margin of exposure 93) — published 2026-05-11, DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10007, PMID 42125559 [S31]. Until a Commission implementing regulation lists it, NMN remains an unauthorised novel food in the EU (status of the authorising act not checked in this pass; open question).
- Investor reading: the EU instrument grants a **time-bounded commercial exclusivity** (data protection) that is not a patent but behaves like an IP right; it belongs in "who owns the rights".

### 1.4 Health Canada — Natural and Non-prescription Health Products (LNHPD)

- **Product licence (NPN).** Licence 80112429 "Tru Niagen", capsule, licence holder Chromadex Inc., licence date 2021-08-26, revised 2026-09-17, sub-submission type "General (non-traditional)" [S32]. The licence carries the authorised medicinal ingredient (nicotinamide riboside 439.0 mg from source material nicotinamide riboside chloride), dose (adults ≥ 18 years, 1 capsule, 1–2 times daily), risk text ("Consult a health care practitioner before using this product…") and **regulator-authorised claims**, e.g. "Clinically proven to increase blood NAD+ levels, to support cellular health." [S32].
- **Two status dimensions**: the web record shows "Licence Status: Active" and "Market status: Not Marketed" for the same NPN [S33]; the API exposes `flag_product_status` but no market status (the `productstatus` endpoint returns 404) [S32]. A regulatory status record needs at least a licence state and a market state.
- **One licence, many names.** Licence 80112465 (New Roots Herbal Inc., NR 250 mg from NRCl) carries eight product names ("Nicotinamide Riboside", "NAD+ Cell Recharge", "NAD+ Lift", …) under one lnhpd_id [S34]. A licence is not a product identity; the seam to D6 is many-to-many.

### 1.5 Courts and patent tribunals

- **Federal Circuit, ChromaDex, Inc. and Trustees of Dartmouth College v. Elysium Health, Inc., No. 2022-1116, decided 2023-02-13**: affirmed summary judgment that the asserted claims of U.S. Patent No. 8,197,807 (claim 1: a composition comprising isolated nicotinamide riboside in combination with tryptophan, nicotinic acid or nicotinamide, formulated for oral administration) are invalid under 35 U.S.C. § 101; records that ChromaDex "licenses the patent from Dartmouth" and sued "a former ChromaDex customer"; the appeal on claim 2 of U.S. 8,383,086 was mooted [S22]. Google Patents simultaneously lists US8197807B2 as "Active , expires 2026-11-19", with the disclaimer that legal status "is an assumption and is not a legal conclusion", and lists PTAB IPR2017-01796 (not instituted) and IPR2021-00491 (final written decision), a Supreme Court docket 23-245, and further district and appellate dockets (including 2025 and 2026 docket numbers not read in this pass) [S21].
- Investor reading: **status lives at the claim level and changes by adjudication**; a patent-level "Active" flag is not an answer to "is the formulation protected".

### 1.6 Other regulators named but not exercised

## 2. Industry (manufacturers, ingredient owners, brands)

### 2.1 Ingredient owners and suppliers

- **Niagen Bioscience, Inc.** (formerly ChromaDex Corp. until 2025-03-10 per EDGAR former names; the FY2025 10-K says the name change was effective March 19, 2025 — fixtures stage) [S28]: "We rely on a single manufacturer for the supply of food-grade NRC." — W.R. Grace & Co.-Conn., exclusive supplier, initial term through 2029-04-30, and "Certain intellectual property held by Grace limits the availability of alternative sources of supply for food-grade NRC." Finished products "are manufactured by third-party, FDA-registered contract manufacturers located in the United States" [S25]. The FY2025 10-K patent table lists 29 U.S. patent numbers with filing, issue and expiry dates and a "Licensor" column (licensed from Cornell University, University of Iowa, Kansas State University; or "Owned by Niagen Bioscience"); the Dartmouth '807 and '086 patents do not appear in that table, but the notes describe the Dartmouth "Exclusive License Agreements" (dated 2012-07-13, amended and restated 2017-03-13 and 2020-12-29, and 2014-05-16) and a Supplemental Agreement of 2024-11-27 under which Dartmouth waived $3.5 million of accrued royalties and fees and no further royalties are payable for the Dartmouth Patents, contingent on an appeal bond for the fee judgment in the Elysium case [S25]. The same notes recount the litigation: complaint filed 2018-09-17 alleging Elysium's BASIS infringes the '807 and '086 patents "held by Dartmouth and licensed exclusively to Niagen Bioscience" [S25]. A licence is therefore a versioned agreement whose economics change after adjudication.
- **Amazentis SA** (urolithin A; consumer brand Mitopure/Timeline): GRAS notice GRN 791 filed 2018-06-15, closed 2018-12-20 with "FDA has no questions" [S08]; the FDA response letter restates Amazentis' manufacturing (two alternative synthesis processes) and specifications (≥ 97 % urolithin A; limits for intermediate A1 0.5 %, impurity AZX12 0.4 %, Cd 0.5 mg/kg, Pb 0.5 mg/kg, As 1.5 mg/kg, Hg 0.1 mg/kg, residual solvents) [S09]. The brand's patent-marking page lists 53 patent and application numbers across 12 jurisdictions without saying which covers which product [S35]; Google Patents lists US9872850B2 (assignee Amazentis SA, priority 2010-12-23, "Active , expires 2032-04-11", claim 1 a method of enhancing muscle performance) [S36].
- **NMN ingredient makers** (Inner Mongolia Kingdomway, SyncoZymes, Willy Chemicals): visible only through FDA NDI letters [S03][S05]; Kingdomway's notification states use "at levels that do not exceed 400 mg NMN per serving or 400 mg NMN per day by adults" [S05].
- **Drug developers whose INDs block supplements**: Metro International Biotech (MIB-626) [S05][S27]; a MetroBiotech comment to the NMN docket is cited by FDA [S03].

### 2.2 Contract manufacturers and brands

- Contract manufacturing is the norm and mostly invisible: Niagen names no contract manufacturer [S25]; a recall names "Supplement Manufacturing Partner Inc." as recalling firm of a spermidine product [S14]; recall records list "Distributed by"/"Manufactured for" text inside `product_description` [S15][S16][S17].
- **Specifications, MMRs and batch records** are required by Part 111 [S29] and are exactly what FDA found missing at Imu-Tek ("you stated that you did not have an MMR") [S10]. They are almost never public; what becomes public is (a) the regulator's restatement of a specification (GRN 791 letter [S09], EU annex [S30]), (b) a COA or test summary, (c) a warning letter describing their absence.
- **Brands' regulatory characterizations**: Tru Niagen's quality page headline is "Accepted by the United States FDA & multiple international regulatory bodies" and states "While the FDA does not regulate the dietary supplement industry, few companies go through the notification process with the FDA. Successful FDA notifications are an extra level of assurance that our key ingredient is safe and effective." [S19]. Set against FDA's own GRAS and NDI texts [S07][S09][S03] and Part 111 [S29], this is the CQ-MF-03 pattern (company characterization vs agency record) in its purest form.
- **Lot-level transparency as a feature**: Tru Niagen offers lot lookup of COAs at a separate transparency site (interactive, needs a lot number; not exercised) and claims "at least 19 internal and third-party tests", ISO/IEC 17025:2017 accredited laboratories, and third-party verification by NSF and "TESTED by SuppCo" [S19].

### 2.3 Market surveillance by competitors (testing as a commercial weapon)

- **ChromaDex, 2021**: 22 NMN products bought on Amazon, HPLC-UV, named brands with label claim vs result; "The remaining fourteen samples (64%) had NMN content below the method's reporting limit or detection limit, which indicates less than 1% of the claimed NMN was present."; ten brands shared one distributor and a common "prominent unknown peak" [S37]. No lot numbers are given. The tester sells a competing NAD+ precursor.
- **Niagen Bioscience, June 2025**: 50 NR products tested between February 2023 and June 2025 by validated HPLC-UV; one brand tested at four intervals below 10 % of label; seven products from unauthorized resellers identified as counterfeit Tru Niagen: "Indicators of counterfeit status included the use of plastic bottles, flip-top caps, multiple label misspellings, yellow powder in the capsules, missing bottle seals or cotton filler, the usage of outdated and out-of-market lot numbers, and other inconsistencies." NMR found undeclared maltodextrin in one counterfeit [S20]. It also states "This analysis did not assess whether the tested products may be infringing on owned or licensed patents." [S20]. Again no lot numbers for most rows.
- Investor reading: these reports are simultaneously **evidence about competitors' products** and **marketing by an interested party**; the graph must record the tester's financial interest (D7 seam) beside each result.

## 3. Testing laboratories and certifiers (who measures, who vouches)

### 3.1 Contract testing labs

- **Eurofins Food Chemistry Testing Madison** issued "Certificate of Analysis" report 3921057-0 (Final, 2022-12-27) for "Orgabay Liposomal NAD 500 mg Supplement", lot LNA-199-0715, Eurofins sample 12344294; the only analysis is "Quantity by Input (QTY_INPUT)", whose method is "Quantity of ingredients verified through batch record review." with NAD+ 500 mg/serving and trimethylglycine (as betaine anhydrous) 250 mg/serving, both reported as "Quantity by Input verified and reviewed from product batch records and ingredient COAs" [S18] (amounts read on the rendered page; the text layer prints each value one row above its label). A document titled COA, from a major lab, with a lot number, that **measures nothing**. This is the decisive evidence for the open question "what classifies a COA vs a test summary": the classification must be per result (measured vs input-verified vs stated), not per document title.
- An unsigned finished-product COA for "Organic Ashwagandha Extract" (manufactured for Elite Source Products, Inc.; lot 2404101ORA, exp 08/2027) mixes visual checks ("Conforms"), ingredient amounts verified by "IPC" (in-process control), an HPLC assay reported only as "Conforms", heavy metals with numeric results per serving (lead 0.380 µg/serving against a 0.5 µg/serving limit), and USP <61>/<62> microbiology [S38]. No issuing laboratory, signer or method validation is named.
- Both COAs are hosted on `m.media-amazon.com`, i.e. they are marketplace compliance uploads [S18][S38].

### 3.2 Independent academic testing

- **Sandalova et al., GeroScience 2024** (PMID 38935229, PMCID PMC11335992, DOI 10.1007/s11357-024-01257-2, CC BY): NMN and urolithin A supplements bought online or in pharmacies were measured by LC-QqQ-MS against their label claims: "Results showed a significant deviation from the labeled amounts, ranging from + 28.6% to -100%." Products are coded NMN#1–18 and UA#1–5, not named (the paper's NMN#11–20 labels a measurement batch, not products); three NMN products were reported "n.q." (not quantifiable): NMN #15 and #16 (−100 % of claim) and NMN #18 (label claim 0) [S39]. Independent and peer-reviewed, but **cannot be joined to a brand or lot**.

### 3.3 Certification bodies

- **NSF Certified for Sport (NSF Guideline 306)** — product listing for Elysium Health "Basis": brand, country of sale, product type, purpose/goal (Energy Enhancement, Pre-Workout, Post-Workout, Sleep Aid), **lot numbers P043-01, M070-01, R212-01**, form, flavor, package size, serving size, label image link [S12b].
- **NSF/ANSI 173 Dietary Supplements** — company listing (Codeage LLC, facility "# 1 USA"): trade designation "Liposomal Nicotinamide Riboside+", Product ID "All", form, serving size, with a scope note ("NSF has tested and Certified that these products contain the identity and quantity of dietary ingredients declared on the product label …") that sits **inside HTML comments and is not displayed** (corrected in the fixtures stage, tool-log E8: do not cite it as the listing's scope); the page header warns "Alert: NSF is concerned about fraudulent downloading and manipulation of website text." and asks users to confirm on the live URL [S13].
- **Informed Sport (LGC)** — product page for Applied Nutrition "Creatine Monohydrate Professional": date certified 05-Aug-2022, goals (Build Muscle, Workout Support), regions, and a **batch table** (24 batch rows on the captured page, each with batch ID, flavour, batch expiration, test date; e.g. H26246, expiration 30 September 2028, test date 30 September 2026), plus a note that one batch "has been tested but is not listed below" [S40].
- Investor/biohacker reading: certifications are **lot- or batch-scoped and dated**, and they certify different things (banned-substance screening vs label identity/quantity vs facility GMP). The certification's scope text is the fact; "certified" alone is not.

## 4. The IP system (patents, licences, marks)

- **Patent records** (Google Patents as aggregator): publication number, application number, inventors, current and original assignee (with a disclaimer), priority date, filing and publication dates, legal status as "assumption", expiry estimate, family members, litigation links (Unified Patents, Darts-ip), claims text [S21][S36].
- **Court opinions**: parties, docket, dates, holding, the claim text at issue [S22].
- **Licence disclosures**: only in securities filings (licensor per patent) [S25] and in court records ("licenses the patent from Dartmouth") [S22]; exclusivity is stated ("licensed exclusively") and the royalty history is disclosed [S25], but field of use and territory were not public in any capture.
- **Patent marking pages**: number lists by jurisdiction, no claim-to-product mapping [S35].
- **Trademarks**: USPTO TSDR answered 401 with the message "Beginning October 2, you’ll need to register for an API key to download bulk data from our TSDR APIs." — no trademark record captured (`_notes/tool-log.md`, response of https://tsdrapi.uspto.gov, 2026-10-08).
- **Licence disclosures over time**: the Dartmouth exclusive licence appears in the court record [S22] and in 10-K notes with its amendment and royalty-waiver history [S25]; that history is the only licence-term information found in public sources.
- Investor reading: for consumer longevity ingredients the realistic IP questions are (a) who asserts ownership or a licence, (b) has any claim been adjudicated, (c) when does the asserted protection expire, (d) is there non-patent exclusivity (EU data protection [S30], exclusive supply with supplier IP [S25]). Claim-by-claim infringement mapping is litigation work, not graph work.

## 5. Trade associations, advocates and marketplaces

- **NPA and ANH** filed the NMN citizen petition and an August 2024 lawsuit; NPA describes FDA's 2025 letter as reversing the 2022 position [S04]. Advocates are interested parties; their pages locate primaries but are not evidence of the agency's position.
- **CRN** petitioned FDA in May 2023 on the drug-preclusion provision (section 201(ff)(3)(B)) generally, with a supplemental filing in March 2025; FDA's 2025-09-29 response denied CRN's petition while confirming NMN is not precluded, per CRN's own release [S42].
- **Marketplaces** (Amazon, eBay, Walmart) are where counterfeits are sold per [S20] and where COAs are uploaded [S18][S38]; D6 owns listings; D8 owns what the lab or certifier said about the lot.

## 7. The four lenses

**Science.** The measurement layer is thin and uneven: validated chromatography (HPLC-UV, LC-QqQ-MS) in competitor and academic surveys [S37][S20][S39]; compendial microbiology and ICP-MS in a finished-product COA [S38]; and "quantity by input" — no measurement — in a major lab's COA [S18]. Label-claim deviation ranges from + 28.6 % to −100 % in the only independent peer-reviewed survey [S39]. Specifications exist in three places: firm-internal (required by Part 111 [S29], rarely public), regulator-restated (GRAS letter [S09], EU annex [S30]) and certifier-scoped (NSF 173's "identity and quantity of dietary ingredients declared on the product label" [S13]). Stability matters: Niagen reports very low NR in high-water formats [S20] — shelf-life vs release limits is a real distinction (OPEN-QUESTIONS quality #2).

**Economics.** Moats in this domain are regulatory and supply-side as much as patent-based: EU data protection to 2025-02-20 [S30]; an exclusive supplier whose own IP "limits the availability of alternative sources" [S25]; a drug IND that, for three years, excluded a competing ingredient (NMN) from the US supplement market until FDA reversed [S05][S03]; a core composition patent whose asserted claims were held invalid [S22]. Recalls and warning letters are direct cost and revenue events (D13 seam). Competitor testing reports are marketing assets [S37][S20].

**Community.** Biohackers verify with COAs, certifier listings and lot lookups [S19][S12b][S40]; counterfeits on marketplaces defeat brand-level trust [S20]; certifier pages carry anti-tampering warnings [S13]; advocacy groups shape regulatory outcomes [S04]. Compounded "longevity injectables" (NAD+, 5-Amino-1MQ, Epithalon) move through 503B facilities that FDA has found non-compliant [S11].

**AI (agent-readiness; scores as in `08-sources-and-tools.md`, which supersedes the research-stage scores).** Agent-ready (3): openFDA enforcement and drugsfda, Europe PMC, ClinicalTrials.gov v2, eCFR (with compression). API or stable document needing custom parsing (2): regulations.gov dockets (200 with browser headers, E7; letter dates can be typos, E5), EUR-Lex, LNHPD (by id only), CAFC opinions, SEC EDGAR filings. Scrape-only (1): FDA warning letters, GRAS inventory, NSF and Informed Sport listings (Informed Sport blocks curl), Google Patents, COA and brand test-report PDFs (table misalignment E3; glyph loss was `pdftotext`'s default encoding, E4). Blocked without registration (0 now): FDA Data Dashboard (inspection classifications, 483 citations) — 401; USPTO TSDR — API key; PatentsView — no response. Interactive only: lot-lookup portals (`transparency.truniagen.com`). Detail and every call: `_notes/tool-log.md`.
