## Green tea: harms and avoidance

Green tea extract is BLOCKED: LiverTox grades green tea with likelihood score A, a well established cause of clinically apparent liver injury, and its case evidence concerns green tea extract [a-gt-likelihood-a] [s-gte-harm]. In a large prospective study cited by LiverTox, ALT elevations occurred in 6.7% of women taking green tea extract and 0.7% of controls; clinically apparent liver injury was not observed in those studies, but the extract was quickly discontinued in patients with ALT elevations [a-gte-alt]. LiverTox describes the prevalence of symptomatic liver injury from green tea extract as not known but undoubtedly low compared with the wide use of these products [a-gte-prevalence-low].

Green tea beverage is not blocked: LiverTox states that drinking green tea has not been associated with liver injury or serum aminotransferase elevations, while noting a small number of cases after drinking green tea infusions [a-gt-beverage-not-associated] [a-gt-infusion-cases].
