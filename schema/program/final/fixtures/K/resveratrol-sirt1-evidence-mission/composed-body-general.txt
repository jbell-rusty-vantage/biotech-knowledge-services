Evidence report (K fixture draft, not published): does resveratrol directly activate SIRT1?
In 2003 Howitz et al. reported that resveratrol lowers the Michaelis constant of SIRT1 for both the acetylated substrate and NAD(+). [a-occ-howitz]
In 2005 Kaeberlein et al. reported that resveratrol is a substrate-specific activator of yeast Sir2 and human SirT1: it enhanced deacetylation of fluorophore-tagged peptides but had no effect on acetylated peptides lacking the fluorophore, and the authors asked for the mechanism behind putative longevity effects to be reexamined. [a-occ-kaeberlein]
In 2010 Pacholec et al. saw no apparent activation of SIRT1 with native peptide or full-length protein substrates, saw activation only with a fluorophore-tagged peptide, and concluded that resveratrol is not a direct activator. [a-occ-pacholec]
In 2013 Hubbard et al. reported that hydrophobic motifs in substrates facilitate activation and that activation depends on SIRT1 Glu230; three of its authors also wrote the 2003 paper, so the two papers count as one source family. [a-occ-hubbard, a-occ-hubbard-refined]
A 2017 rat study that presupposed resveratrol is a SIRT1 activator was retracted in 2025 and is not counted as evidence. [a-sah-occ-presup]
Verdict on the general proposition: MIXED_OR_CONFLICTING; the qualified proposition is judged in a separate report. [adj-sup-general, as-refines]
